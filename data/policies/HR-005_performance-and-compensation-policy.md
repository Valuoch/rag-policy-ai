## 1. Purpose

This policy describes how Lumora Systems, Inc. ("Lumora" or "the company") sets expectations, gives feedback, evaluates performance, and pays employees. It explains the annual review cycle, the rating scale, how merit increases, promotions and bonuses are decided, and what happens when performance falls below expectations.

Lumora's goals for performance management and compensation are:

- Clear expectations. Every employee should know what good work looks like in their role and how it is measured.
- Frequent, honest feedback. Feedback should happen all year, not only at review time.
- Fair and consistent decisions. Ratings and pay decisions are calibrated across teams so that similar performance is treated similarly.
- Competitive and transparent pay. Pay is tied to the market, to the role, and to performance, and salary ranges are shared openly.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all regular full-time and part-time employees of Lumora in all US locations, including the Austin, Texas headquarters, the Raleigh, North Carolina office, and remote employees in other US states.

This policy does not apply to:

- Interns and temporary employees, who receive feedback through their program coordinator and are not eligible for merit increases or the annual bonus plan.
- Contractors and agency workers, who are managed under their contract terms.
- Sales roles on a commission plan. Sales employees participate in the review cycle and merit process described here, but variable pay is governed by their individual sales compensation plan instead of section 13.

Where state law gives employees greater rights than this policy, state law controls.

## 3. Definitions

- Base salary: The employee's annual rate of pay, excluding bonuses, commissions, stipends and benefits.
- Level: The job level assigned to a role in Lumora's career framework. Individual contributor (IC) levels run from L1 (entry) to L5 (principal). Management levels are Manager, Director and Vice President (VP).
- Pay band: The minimum-to-maximum salary range for a given job family and level.
- Compa-ratio: An employee's base salary divided by the midpoint of their pay band. A compa-ratio of 1.00 means the employee is paid at the midpoint.
- Calibration: A meeting in which managers and their leaders review proposed ratings together to ensure consistent standards across teams.
- Merit increase: An increase to base salary based on performance and position in the pay band.
- Promotion: A move to a higher level, either in the same job family or a different one.
- Performance Improvement Plan (PIP): A formal, written plan that identifies performance gaps and the actions needed to close them within a set period.
- Review cycle: The annual process of self-review, manager review, calibration and rating delivery.

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

Employees are responsible for:

- Understanding their role expectations and goals.
- Preparing for and attending regular 1:1s with their manager.
- Completing the self-review in Workday on time.
- Asking for feedback and raising concerns early.

Managers are responsible for:

- Holding 1:1s with each direct report at least every 2 weeks.
- Setting and updating goals with each direct report.
- Writing fair, evidence-based reviews and attending calibration.
- Delivering ratings and pay decisions personally and explaining the reasons.
- Consulting their HR Business Partner before starting a PIP.

HR (People team) is responsible for:

- Running the review cycle in Workday and publishing the timeline.
- Facilitating calibration sessions.
- Maintaining the career framework and pay bands.
- Reviewing pay decisions for equity and consistency.

The Chief People Officer owns this policy. The CFO approves the annual merit and bonus budgets.

## 5. Ongoing Feedback and 1:1 Meetings

Performance management at Lumora is continuous. The formal review cycle summarizes the year; it should never be the first time an employee hears about a concern.

### 5.1 1:1 meetings

Every employee must have a 1:1 meeting with their manager at least every 2 weeks. Many teams meet weekly. A 1:1 should cover progress on goals, obstacles, feedback in both directions, and career development. If a 1:1 is cancelled, it should be rescheduled within the same two-week window.

Managers should keep brief notes of 1:1 discussions, especially when they discuss performance concerns. Notes can be kept in the Workday "Check-ins" feature so that they are available at review time.

### 5.2 Feedback

Employees may request feedback from peers at any time through Workday. Managers are encouraged to give specific, timely feedback that names the behavior, its impact, and what to continue or change.

## 6. Goal Setting

Goals are set at the start of each calendar year, during or soon after the January review conversation, and are recorded in Workday by January 31. Each employee should have 3 to 5 goals that connect to their team and company objectives. Goals should be specific and measurable where possible.

Goals can be updated at any time when priorities change. The mid-year check-in in June is the standard point to confirm or revise goals for the second half of the year.

## 7. Annual Review Cycle

Lumora runs one annual performance review cycle covering the calendar year. The cycle has the following stages:

### 7.1 Self-review (December)

In December, each employee completes a self-review in Workday. The self-review asks the employee to summarize accomplishments against goals, describe how they demonstrated Lumora's values, and identify development areas. HR opens the self-review form in early December and publishes the exact due date each year.

### 7.2 Manager review (January)

In January, managers write a review for each direct report. The manager review considers the self-review, peer feedback, goal results and the manager's own observations from 1:1s. Managers propose a rating on the 1 to 5 scale described in section 8.

### 7.3 Calibration (January)

Calibration also takes place in January, after proposed ratings are entered. Managers present proposed ratings to their leader and peer managers, with an HR Business Partner facilitating. Ratings may change during calibration. The final rating is the calibrated rating, not the manager's initial proposal.

### 7.4 Rating conversations

After calibration, managers hold a review conversation with each employee to share the final rating, the written review and the key themes. Pay decisions (merit and bonus) are communicated separately once budgets are finalized, before merit increases take effect on April 1 and before the bonus is paid in March.

### 7.5 Mid-year check-in (June)

In June, every manager and employee complete a mid-year check-in. The mid-year check-in does not produce a rating and does not affect pay directly. It is a structured conversation about progress against goals, feedback, and development plans. It is recorded in Workday.

### 7.6 Eligibility for a rating

Employees who start on or after October 1 are generally not rated in that year's cycle because there is not enough time to evaluate their work; they complete their 30/60/90-day check-ins under HR-008 instead. Employees on an approved leave of absence for a large part of the year are rated on the period they were working.

## 8. Rating Scale

Lumora uses a five-point rating scale:

| Rating | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does not meet | Performance is consistently below the expectations of the role. Immediate improvement is required. |
| 2 | Partially meets | Performance meets some expectations but falls short in important areas. |
| 3 | Meets | Performance fully meets the expectations of the role. This is solid, valued performance. |
| 4 | Exceeds | Performance regularly goes beyond the expectations of the role. |
| 5 | Exceptional | Performance is far beyond expectations and has outstanding impact on the team or company. |

A rating of 3 (Meets) is a positive rating. It means the employee is doing the job well. Lumora does not use a forced distribution, but calibration groups review the overall spread of ratings to check for consistency.

## 9. Calibration Principles

During calibration, participants must:

- Base ratings on evidence from the full review period, not only recent events.
- Compare performance against the expectations for the employee's level, not against other employees' personalities.
- Avoid considering protected characteristics, leave taken under HR-002, or any complaint made in good faith under HR-007.
- Keep calibration discussions confidential.

HR checks calibrated ratings for patterns that might indicate bias, such as differences in average rating by gender, race or leave status, and raises any concerns with the relevant leader before ratings are final.

## 10. Compensation Philosophy and Pay Bands

### 10.1 Philosophy

Lumora aims to pay competitively for each role in the US market. Base salary depends on the role's level and job family, the employee's skills and experience, and their sustained performance.

### 10.2 Pay bands

Every role is assigned to a pay band. Pay bands are reviewed annually by the People team using external market data, and updated bands are published in Workday. A band review may move a band's minimum, midpoint and maximum. If a band minimum increases above an employee's salary, the employee's salary is raised to the new minimum on April 1.

### 10.3 Pay transparency

Lumora includes the salary range in all job postings, both internal and external. Employees can see the pay band for their own role in Workday and can ask HR for the pay band of any role they are interested in.

### 10.4 Pay equity

The People team runs a pay equity analysis each year before merit decisions are finalized. Unexplained pay gaps are corrected through equity adjustments, which take effect with merit increases on April 1 or sooner if approved.

## 11. Merit Increases

### 11.1 Timing

Merit increases are effective April 1 each year. Increases appear in the first pay period that begins on or after April 1. Lumora pays semi-monthly, on the 15th and the last business day of each month, so the first paycheck reflecting a merit increase is normally the April 15 paycheck.

### 11.2 How merit is decided

The CFO and Chief People Officer set the company-wide merit budget each year. Managers recommend individual increases within their team's budget using a merit guideline that considers:

- The employee's final calibrated rating.
- The employee's position in their pay band (compa-ratio). Employees lower in the band generally receive a larger percentage increase than employees near the top of the band for the same rating.
- Internal equity with peers in the same role and level.

Employees with a rating of 1 (Does not meet) are not eligible for a merit increase. Employees with a rating of 2 (Partially meets) are generally not eligible, except where required to bring pay up to the band minimum.

### 11.3 Employees at band maximum

An employee whose salary is at the maximum of their band may receive a lump-sum payment instead of a base salary increase, at the manager's recommendation and HR's approval.

### 11.4 New hires

Employees hired on or after October 1 of the prior year are generally not eligible for a merit increase in the following April, because their starting salary was recently set at market.

## 12. Promotions

### 12.1 Promotion windows

Promotions are considered twice a year, with effective dates of April 1 and October 1. Promotion nominations for the April 1 window are reviewed during January calibration. Nominations for the October 1 window are reviewed in August.

### 12.2 Criteria

A promotion requires that the employee:

- Is consistently performing at the expectations of the next level, as described in the career framework.
- Has a most recent rating of 3 (Meets) or higher.
- Is not on a PIP.
- Fills a business need for a role at the next level, which the leader confirms with HR and Finance.

Time in level alone is not a reason for promotion.

### 12.3 Promotion increase

A promotion normally includes a base salary increase that places the employee at least at the minimum of the new pay band. If the promotion is to a role with a higher bonus target (for example from L3 to L4, or from an individual contributor role to Manager), the new bonus target applies from the promotion effective date and the bonus for that year is prorated between the two targets.

### 12.4 Internal transfers

Lateral moves to another team at the same level can happen at any time and are not limited to the promotion windows. An internal applicant must have been in their current role for at least 12 months and must not be on a PIP, unless HR approves an exception.

## 13. Annual Bonus Plan

### 13.1 Bonus targets

Eligible employees participate in the annual bonus plan. Bonus targets are a percentage of base salary as follows:

| Role level | Annual bonus target (% of base salary) |
|---|---|
| Individual contributors, levels L1 to L3 | 8% |
| Individual contributors, levels L4 to L5 | 10% |
| Managers | 15% |
| Directors | 20% |
| Vice Presidents | 30% |

### 13.2 Funding and individual payout

The bonus pool is funded based on company performance against annual financial goals approved by the board. Individual payouts depend on the funded pool and the employee's calibrated rating. An employee with a rating of 1 (Does not meet) does not receive a bonus. The target is not a guarantee; actual payouts can be below or above target.

### 13.3 Payment timing

The annual bonus for a performance year is paid in March of the following year through regular payroll. For example, the bonus for performance year 2025 is paid in March 2026.

### 13.4 Employment on payout date

An employee must be employed by Lumora on the bonus payout date to receive the bonus. An employee who resigns or is terminated before the payout date does not receive a bonus for the prior year, unless state law or a written agreement requires otherwise.

### 13.5 Proration for new hires

Employees hired after January 1 receive a prorated bonus based on the number of days employed during the performance year. Employees hired after October 1 are not eligible for a bonus for that performance year; they become eligible starting the next calendar year.

Example: An L2 engineer with a base salary of $100,000 is hired on July 1. The bonus target is 8%, or $8,000 for a full year. Because the engineer worked about half the year, the prorated target is about $4,000, which is then adjusted for company funding and the engineer's rating.

### 13.6 Leaves of absence

Time on paid parental leave under HR-002 counts as time worked for bonus proration. Time on other unpaid leave may reduce the prorated bonus, as permitted by law.

## 14. Performance Improvement Plans (PIPs)

### 14.1 When a PIP is used

A PIP is used when an employee's performance does not meet the expectations of the role and informal feedback has not resolved the issue. A PIP is not a disciplinary action for misconduct; conduct issues are handled under HR-001 and HR-007.

Before starting a PIP, the manager must have discussed the concerns with the employee in 1:1s and must consult the HR Business Partner, who reviews the documentation.

### 14.2 Length and check-ins

A PIP lasts 60 days. During the PIP, the manager and employee hold check-ins every 2 weeks to review progress against the plan. Each check-in is documented in Workday. HR may attend.

### 14.3 Contents of a PIP

Every PIP must include:

- The specific performance gaps, with examples.
- Clear, measurable expectations the employee must meet.
- The support the company will provide, such as training, coaching or more frequent feedback.
- The check-in schedule.
- The possible outcomes at the end of the plan.

### 14.4 Outcomes

At the end of the 60 days, the outcome is one of the following:

- Successful completion: the employee has met the expectations and returns to normal performance management.
- Extension: in limited cases where significant progress has been made, HR may approve one extension of up to 30 days.
- Separation: if expectations are not met, employment may be terminated, following the offboarding procedures in HR-008.

While on a PIP, an employee is not eligible for promotion or internal transfer.

## 15. Pay Administration

- Payroll: Lumora pays semi-monthly on the 15th and the last business day of each month, for 24 pay periods per year. If a pay date falls on a weekend or holiday, pay is issued on the preceding business day.
- Pay records: Employees can view pay statements, salary history and bonus targets in Workday.
- Off-cycle adjustments: Increases outside the April 1 merit cycle (for example, a counter-offer, a market adjustment or an equity correction) require approval from the department VP and the Chief People Officer.
- Errors: Employees who believe their pay is wrong should contact hr@lumora.example. Underpayments are corrected no later than the next regular pay date.
- Confidentiality: Employees are free to discuss their own pay with others. Lumora does not prohibit or retaliate against pay discussions.

## 16. Disagreeing With a Rating

An employee who disagrees with their final rating may submit a written comment in Workday within 14 days of the rating conversation. The comment is attached to the review record. The employee may also ask their HR Business Partner for a review meeting with the manager's leader. Ratings are changed only if the review finds an error in process or evidence.

## 17. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: When are merit increases effective?
A: Merit increases are effective April 1 each year, and normally first appear in the April 15 paycheck.

Q: What is the annual bonus target for a manager?
A: Managers have a bonus target of 15% of base salary. Directors are 20%, VPs are 30%, individual contributors at L4 to L5 are 10%, and individual contributors at L1 to L3 are 8%.

Q: If I resign in February, will I get last year's bonus?
A: No. The bonus is paid in March and you must be employed on the payout date to receive it.

Q: I joined Lumora on November 3. Am I eligible for a bonus this year?
A: No. Employees hired after October 1 are not eligible for a bonus in their first performance year. You will be eligible starting next calendar year.

Q: How often can I be promoted?
A: Promotions are considered twice a year, effective April 1 and October 1. There is no fixed minimum time in level, but you must be performing at the next level and have a rating of 3 or higher.

Q: How long is a Performance Improvement Plan?
A: A PIP lasts 60 days, with check-ins every 2 weeks.

Q: Is a rating of 3 bad?
A: No. A 3 means "Meets" and indicates you are fully doing the job well.

Q: How often should I meet with my manager?
A: You should have a 1:1 with your manager at least every 2 weeks.
