## 1. Purpose

Lumora Systems, Inc. ("Lumora") is committed to a workplace where everyone is treated with dignity and respect, and where employment decisions are based on merit. This policy:

- States Lumora's commitment to equal employment opportunity.
- Prohibits discrimination, harassment (including sexual harassment) and retaliation.
- Sets standards for workplace conduct, including relationships, workplace violence, and alcohol and drugs.
- Explains how to report concerns and how Lumora investigates them.

This policy supports and expands on the Code of Conduct in HR-001.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to everyone who works for or with Lumora, including employees at every level, interns, temporary workers and contractors. It also protects employees from harassment by customers, vendors and visitors.

The policy applies wherever Lumora work happens, including:

- Lumora offices in Austin and Raleigh, and any other company premises.
- Home offices and other remote work locations.
- Business travel, client sites, conferences and company events, including after-hours events.
- Virtual spaces, including Slack, email, video calls and other company systems.
- Personal social media or messaging, when the conduct affects the workplace or involves colleagues.

## 3. Definitions

- Protected characteristic: Race, color, religion, creed, sex, pregnancy (including childbirth, lactation and related conditions), sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, national origin, ancestry, age (40 and over), physical or mental disability, genetic information, marital status, military or veteran status, citizenship status, and any other characteristic protected by federal, state or local law.
- Discrimination: Treating a person less favorably in any term or condition of employment because of a protected characteristic.
- Harassment: Unwelcome conduct based on a protected characteristic that is severe or pervasive enough to create an intimidating, hostile or offensive work environment, or that becomes a condition of employment. Lumora's standard is stricter than the law: conduct can violate this policy even if it is not severe or pervasive enough to be illegal.
- Sexual harassment: Unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, and other verbal, visual or physical conduct of a sexual nature, including when submission to such conduct is made a condition of employment or used as a basis for employment decisions.
- Bullying: Repeated, unreasonable behavior directed at an employee that a reasonable person would find intimidating, humiliating or undermining, whether or not it relates to a protected characteristic.
- Retaliation: Any adverse action taken against someone because they reported a concern in good faith, participated in an investigation, or opposed conduct they reasonably believed violated this policy.
- Complainant: The person who reports a concern or who is the subject of the alleged conduct.
- Respondent: The person whose conduct is the subject of a report.

## 4. Equal Employment Opportunity

Lumora provides equal employment opportunity to all employees and applicants. Lumora does not discriminate on the basis of any protected characteristic in any aspect of employment, including recruiting, hiring, job assignment, compensation, performance ratings, promotion, training, discipline, benefits, and termination.

### 4.1 Hiring and pay practices

Lumora supports equal opportunity through these practices:

- Salary ranges are included in all job postings, as described in HR-005.
- Structured interviews with consistent questions and scoring guides are used for all roles.
- Performance ratings are calibrated and reviewed by HR for patterns of bias, and an annual pay equity analysis is performed (HR-005).

### 4.2 Reasonable accommodation

Lumora provides reasonable accommodations to qualified employees and applicants with disabilities, to employees with pregnancy-related limitations, and for sincerely held religious beliefs and practices, unless the accommodation would cause undue hardship.

To request an accommodation, contact HR at hr@lumora.example or through the "Accommodation Request" form in Workday. Employees do not need to use any special words. HR will engage in an interactive process with the employee, which may include asking for medical documentation of the limitation (not the diagnosis). Medical information is kept confidential and stored separately from the personnel file, in line with LEG-001.

Examples of accommodations include adjusted schedules, ergonomic equipment, a private lactation space, time off for medical appointments, remote work for a period of time under HR-004, or time off for religious observance.

## 5. Prohibited Harassment

### 5.1 Examples of harassment

Harassment can be verbal, written, visual, physical or digital. Examples include:

- Slurs, epithets, insults, or jokes about a protected characteristic.
- Mocking someone's accent, religious dress, disability or appearance.
- Displaying or sharing offensive images, memes, GIFs or emoji in Slack, email or on screens.
- Threats, intimidation or hostile acts based on a protected characteristic.
- Deliberately and repeatedly using the wrong name or pronouns for a colleague after being informed of the correct ones.

### 5.2 Examples of sexual harassment

- Unwanted sexual advances, propositions or repeated requests for dates after a person has said no.
- Comments about a person's body, sexual jokes or sexual innuendo.
- Unwanted touching, hugging, blocking a person's path, or standing too close.
- Sending sexual messages, images or links, including through personal phones.
- Offering a benefit (such as a promotion or a better assignment) in exchange for sexual favors, or threatening a negative consequence for refusing.

Sexual harassment can occur between people of any gender, and between people of the same gender.

### 5.3 Bullying and abusive conduct

Lumora also prohibits bullying and abusive conduct that is not based on a protected characteristic, such as shouting at colleagues, public humiliation, persistent unjustified criticism, sabotage of work, and deliberate exclusion from meetings or information needed to do the job. Ordinary management actions, such as giving honest feedback, assigning work, or placing an employee on a PIP under HR-005, are not bullying when done respectfully.

## 6. Workplace Conduct Standards

### 6.1 Professional behavior

Employees are expected to treat colleagues, customers and partners with courtesy and respect, in person and online. Disagreements should focus on ideas, not people.

### 6.2 Workplace relationships

Lumora does not prohibit consensual romantic relationships between employees, but the following rules apply:

- A romantic or sexual relationship between a manager and a direct or indirect report must be disclosed to HR. HR will work with those involved to remove the reporting relationship, for example by changing the reporting line, so that the manager does not make or influence decisions about the other person's pay, rating, promotion or assignments.
- Any relationship that creates an actual or potential conflict of interest must be disclosed under the conflicts of interest rules in HR-001.
- Employees must not pursue a colleague romantically after the colleague has indicated that the interest is not welcome.
- Public displays of affection at work, or conduct that makes others uncomfortable, are not appropriate.

Disclosures are kept confidential and shared only with those who need to know to manage the conflict.

### 6.3 Workplace violence and weapons

Workplace violence, including threats, physical assault, stalking, intimidation and destruction of property, is prohibited. Weapons, including firearms, are prohibited on company premises, at company events and while conducting Lumora business, to the extent permitted by state law. Where state law allows employees to keep a lawfully owned firearm locked in a personal vehicle in a parking area, Lumora follows that law.

If anyone is in immediate danger, call 911 first. Then report the incident to your manager and to HR. Threats received by email, Slack or social media should also be reported to Security at security@lumora.example so that the message can be preserved.

### 6.4 Alcohol and drugs

Employees must not work while impaired by alcohol, cannabis or illegal drugs. Possession, sale or use of illegal drugs while working or on company premises is prohibited. Alcohol may be served only at company events approved by a VP or above, and employees who drink must do so in moderation. Alcohol is not permitted during regular work in the office. Employees taking prescription medication that may affect safe work should speak to HR about a possible accommodation. Rules on alcohol expenses are in FIN-001.

### 6.5 Dress code

The standard is business casual in the office, and client-appropriate attire when meeting customers. Requests to adjust the dress code for religious, disability or medical reasons are handled as accommodation requests under section 4.2.

## 7. Reporting Concerns

### 7.1 Who should report

Any employee who experiences or witnesses conduct that may violate this policy is encouraged to report it promptly. Managers have a duty to report: a manager who receives a report, or observes possible harassment, discrimination or retaliation, must notify HR within 1 business day, even if the employee asks the manager to keep it private.

### 7.2 How to report

Employees can report to any of the following:

- Their manager, or any other manager they trust.
- HR, by emailing hr@lumora.example or speaking with any HR Business Partner.
- The Ethics & Compliance hotline at 1-800-555-0142, which is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and accepts anonymous reports.

Employees are not required to report to their own manager first, and should never be required to report to the person they are complaining about.

### 7.3 Anonymous reports

Anonymous reports made through the Ethics & Compliance hotline are taken seriously and investigated as fully as possible. The hotline provides a report number so that the reporter can check status and answer follow-up questions without revealing their identity. Anonymous reports can be harder to investigate if investigators cannot ask follow-up questions, so reporters are encouraged to check back.

### 7.4 What to include

A helpful report includes what happened, when and where, who was involved, any witnesses, and any evidence such as screenshots, emails or Slack messages. Employees should not delete messages that may be relevant.

### 7.5 External agencies

Employees also have the right to file a complaint with the US Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) or a state agency such as the Texas Workforce Commission Civil Rights Division or the North Carolina Office of Administrative Hearings, Civil Rights Division. Nothing in this policy limits that right.

## 8. Investigation Process

### 8.1 Timeline

Lumora begins an investigation within 5 business days of receiving a report. The target is to complete investigations within 30 days. Complex cases may take longer; when they do, the investigator updates the complainant and respondent on the expected timing.

### 8.2 Steps

1. Intake: HR records the report, acknowledges it to the reporter, and assesses whether interim measures are needed.
2. Interim measures: Where appropriate, HR may separate the parties, change schedules or reporting lines, or place the respondent on paid administrative leave. Interim measures are not a finding of wrongdoing and must not disadvantage the complainant.
3. Investigator assignment: An HR investigator who has no conflict of interest is assigned. For allegations involving a VP or above, or where HR has a conflict, the General Counsel may appoint an external investigator.
4. Interviews and evidence: The investigator interviews the complainant, the respondent and relevant witnesses, and reviews documents and electronic records. Under SEC-002, Lumora may review company systems such as email and Slack as part of an investigation.
5. Findings: The investigator decides whether a violation occurred, based on the preponderance of the evidence (more likely than not).
6. Outcome: The complainant and respondent are told whether the policy was violated. Details of any discipline are shared only with those who need to know.

### 8.3 Confidentiality

Investigations are kept as confidential as possible, consistent with a thorough investigation and legal requirements. Participants are asked not to discuss the investigation in a way that could interfere with it, but this does not restrict employees' legal rights to discuss terms and conditions of employment. Investigation records are retained for 7 years after the employee's separation, consistent with the HR record retention rules in LEG-001.

### 8.4 Cooperation

Employees must cooperate fully and honestly with investigations. Providing false information on purpose, or destroying evidence, is a violation of this policy.

## 9. Non-Retaliation

Lumora strictly prohibits retaliation. No one may be disciplined, demoted, given a lower rating, excluded, threatened or otherwise treated adversely because they:

- Reported a concern in good faith, even if the investigation does not confirm a violation.
- Participated as a witness in an investigation.
- Requested an accommodation.
- Filed a complaint with a government agency.

Retaliation is a separate violation, and can be found even when the original complaint is not substantiated. Retaliation concerns can be reported through any channel in section 7.2. Knowingly making a false report is not protected and may lead to discipline.

## 10. Corrective Action

When a violation is found, Lumora takes prompt corrective action proportional to the seriousness of the conduct. Actions may include coaching, required training, a written warning, removal of management responsibilities, reduction or loss of bonus eligibility, transfer, and termination of employment. For contractors and vendors, Lumora may end the contract or remove the individual from Lumora work. Managers who knew of harassment and failed to report it may also face discipline.

## 11. Training

All employees must complete anti-harassment training within 30 days of hire, as part of the onboarding trainings listed in HR-008, and then every year. Managers complete an additional manager-specific module each year covering how to receive and escalate reports and how to prevent retaliation. Training is assigned and tracked in Workday. Some states have additional training content requirements, which Lumora includes for employees in those states.

## 12. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I report anonymously?
A: Yes. Call the Ethics & Compliance hotline at 1-800-555-0142, available 24/7. You will receive a report number to follow up without giving your name.

Q: How quickly will Lumora act on my report?
A: An investigation begins within 5 business days of the report, and Lumora aims to complete it within 30 days.

Q: I'm dating someone on another team. Do I need to tell anyone?
A: Only if there is a reporting relationship or a conflict of interest. A relationship between a manager and a direct or indirect report must be disclosed to HR.

Q: My manager told me a colleague's joke "wasn't that bad" and did nothing. What can I do?
A: You can report directly to HR at hr@lumora.example or through the hotline. Managers are required to notify HR within 1 business day of any report, and failure to do so is itself a policy violation.

Q: Does this policy cover a customer harassing me on a video call?
A: Yes. Harassment by customers, vendors and visitors is covered. Report it, and Lumora will take steps such as reassigning the account contact.

Q: Can I bring a drink to my desk on a Friday afternoon?
A: No. Alcohol is allowed only at approved company events, in moderation.

Q: Will I get in trouble if my report is not proven?
A: No. Reports made in good faith are protected even if no violation is found. Only knowingly false reports can lead to discipline.
