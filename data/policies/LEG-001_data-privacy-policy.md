## 1. Purpose

Lumora Systems, Inc. ("Lumora") collects and uses personal data about our customers' users, our employees, job candidates, and business contacts. This Data Privacy & Protection Policy sets out how Lumora collects, uses, shares, stores, and deletes personal data so that we respect individuals' privacy rights and comply with applicable privacy laws.

Lumora applies the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the California Consumer Privacy Act as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act (CCPA/CPRA), and other applicable privacy and data protection laws. Where laws differ, Lumora follows the stricter requirement unless the Privacy team advises otherwise.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All personal data processed by Lumora in any format (electronic, paper, audio, or video), whether Lumora acts as a controller (deciding why and how data is processed, such as employee data) or as a processor (processing customer data on a customer's instructions in the Lumora analytics platform).
- All Lumora workforce members, including employees, contractors, interns, and temporary workers.
- All systems that store or process personal data, including Workday, SAP Concur, Navan, ServiceNow, Slack, email, the Lumora product platform, and vendor systems.
- All vendors and subprocessors that handle personal data on Lumora's behalf.

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities

### 3.1 Data Protection Officer (DPO)

The General Counsel serves as Data Protection Officer (DPO). The current DPO is Priya Raman. The DPO owns this policy, advises on privacy law, oversees privacy impact assessments, manages regulator communications, and decides whether a personal data breach must be notified to regulators or individuals.

### 3.2 Privacy team

The Privacy team, reachable at privacy@lumora.example, handles data subject requests, reviews privacy impact assessments, maintains the record of processing activities, negotiates Data Processing Agreements, and runs annual privacy training.

### 3.3 Security team

The Security team, led by the CISO, protects personal data through the technical and organizational controls in SEC-001 Information Security Policy and leads investigation of security incidents.

### 3.4 Data owners and managers

Business leaders who own a system or process that uses personal data must make sure it complies with this policy, request a privacy impact assessment before launching new processing, and enforce retention schedules.

### 3.5 All workforce members

Everyone must handle personal data only as needed for their job, follow this policy, complete annual privacy training, and report data subject requests and suspected breaches promptly.

## 4. Definitions

- **Personal data**: Any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. Examples include names, email addresses, phone numbers, IP addresses, device identifiers, employee IDs, and location data. Under CCPA/CPRA this is called "personal information."
- **Sensitive personal data**: Personal data that requires extra protection, such as government ID numbers, financial account numbers, health information, precise geolocation, racial or ethnic origin, and biometric data.
- **Data subject**: The individual the personal data is about.
- **Processing**: Any operation performed on personal data, including collecting, storing, using, sharing, and deleting it.
- **Controller**: The organization that decides why and how personal data is processed.
- **Processor**: An organization that processes personal data on behalf of a controller.
- **Subprocessor / vendor**: A third party that processes personal data on Lumora's behalf.
- **Data subject request (DSR)**: A request from an individual to exercise privacy rights, such as access, deletion, or correction.
- **Personal data breach**: A security incident that leads to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or access to personal data.
- **Privacy impact assessment (PIA)**: A structured review of a planned processing activity to identify and reduce privacy risks.
- **Data Processing Agreement (DPA)**: A contract that sets out how a vendor must protect and process personal data on Lumora's behalf.

In Lumora's data classification scheme (SEC-001), personal data (PII) is classified as Restricted.

## 5. Privacy Principles

Lumora processes personal data according to the following principles:

1. **Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency**: We process personal data only with a valid legal basis and explain our practices in clear privacy notices.
2. **Purpose limitation**: We collect personal data for specified, legitimate purposes and do not use it for unrelated purposes without a new legal basis.
3. **Data minimization**: We collect only the personal data we need.
4. **Accuracy**: We keep personal data accurate and up to date, and correct it when asked.
5. **Storage limitation**: We keep personal data only as long as needed, following the retention schedule in Section 9.
6. **Integrity and confidentiality**: We protect personal data with appropriate security controls (SEC-001).
7. **Accountability**: We document our processing and can demonstrate compliance.

## 6. Collecting and Using Personal Data

### 6.1 Customer data

Customers upload data to the Lumora analytics platform. For this data Lumora generally acts as a processor and may use it only to provide the services described in the customer contract and the customer's DPA. Employees must not access customer data except as needed to provide support or operate the service, and only with authorized access.

### 6.2 Employee and candidate data

Lumora processes employee data in Workday and related HR, payroll, benefits, and expense systems to manage employment. Candidate data is processed in recruiting systems to evaluate applications. Access to HR data is limited to HR, payroll, and managers with a need to know.

### 6.3 Business contact and marketing data

Lumora processes business contact information for sales and marketing. Marketing emails must include an unsubscribe option, and opt-out requests must be honored.

### 6.4 Using personal data in non-production environments

Production personal data must not be copied into development, test, or demo environments unless it has been anonymized or the Privacy team has approved the use. Use synthetic data wherever possible.

### 6.5 Personal data and AI tools

Personal data must never be entered into unapproved or public AI tools. Only approved tools listed in SEC-002 IT Acceptable Use Policy may be used, and only for appropriate purposes.

## 7. Data Subject Rights and Requests

### 7.1 Rights

Depending on applicable law and where they live, individuals may have the right to:

- Access the personal data Lumora holds about them.
- Correct inaccurate personal data.
- Delete personal data.
- Receive their data in a portable format.
- Object to or restrict certain processing.
- Opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information (CCPA/CPRA). Lumora does not sell personal information.

### 7.2 Response deadline

Data subject requests (access, deletion, and correction) are answered within 30 days of receipt. Where a law permits an extension for complex requests, the Privacy team will inform the requester before the original deadline.

### 7.3 Forwarding requests

Any employee who receives a data subject request must forward it to privacy@lumora.example within 2 business days of receipt. A request may arrive in any form: an email to a salesperson, a support ticket, a message on social media, or a phone call. It does not need to use the words "data subject request." Do not respond to the requester yourself, other than to confirm that the request has been passed to the Privacy team.

### 7.4 How the Privacy team handles a request

1. Log the request and its receipt date.
2. Verify the requester's identity using a method proportionate to the sensitivity of the data.
3. Determine whether Lumora is the controller or the processor. If Lumora is the processor for a customer, the request is referred to that customer, and Lumora assists as required by the DPA.
4. Locate the relevant personal data across systems with help from data owners.
5. Fulfill the request or document a lawful reason for refusing it (for example, a legal obligation to retain records).
6. Respond to the requester within 30 days and record the outcome.

### 7.5 Requests from customers about their users

Customers may ask Lumora to help them respond to their own users' requests. Support teams should route these to the Privacy team within the same 2 business day window.

## 8. Privacy Impact Assessments and Privacy by Design

### 8.1 When a PIA is required

A privacy impact assessment (PIA) is required before launching any new processing of personal data or onboarding any new vendor that will handle personal data. Examples include:

- A new product feature that collects or analyzes personal data.
- A new HR or recruiting tool.
- A new marketing campaign that uses a new data source.
- Using existing personal data for a new purpose.
- Any processing of sensitive personal data.

### 8.2 PIA process

1. The project owner submits a PIA request through the Lumora Help ServiceNow portal, describing the data, purpose, systems, vendors, and data flows.
2. The Privacy team reviews legal basis, necessity, retention, and data subject rights.
3. The Security team reviews technical controls under SEC-001.
4. The Privacy team records required mitigations. The project must not launch until the PIA is approved.

### 8.3 Privacy by design

Teams must build privacy into products and processes from the start: collect the minimum data, set privacy-protective defaults, limit access, and define retention before launch.

## 9. Data Retention and Deletion

### 9.1 Retention schedule

| Data category | Retention period |
|---|---|
| Customer data | Deleted within 90 days after contract end |
| Employee HR records | Kept 7 years after separation |
| Recruiting data of unsuccessful candidates | Kept 2 years |
| Financial records | Kept 7 years |

### 9.2 Applying the schedule

- Data owners must configure systems to delete or anonymize data when the retention period ends.
- Customer data is deleted from production systems and backups within 90 days after the customer's contract ends, unless the contract or law requires otherwise.
- Records subject to a legal hold issued by Legal must not be deleted until the hold is lifted, even if the retention period has passed.
- Data with no specific retention period must be kept only as long as there is a documented business need; consult the Privacy team.

### 9.3 Secure disposal

Personal data must be disposed of securely: electronic data by approved deletion or wiping methods, and paper records by locked shredding bins. Devices are wiped by IT before reuse or disposal.

## 10. Sharing Personal Data and Vendor Management

### 10.1 Data Processing Agreements

Vendors handling personal data must sign a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) before they receive any personal data. The DPA must require the vendor to process data only on Lumora's instructions, protect it with appropriate security, notify Lumora of breaches, assist with data subject requests, and delete or return data when the service ends.

### 10.2 Vendor onboarding

Before a vendor that handles personal data is engaged, the business owner must complete a PIA (Section 8), Security must complete a vendor security review, and Legal must execute the DPA. Procurement will not issue a purchase order until all three are complete.

### 10.3 International transfers

Personal data transferred outside the country where it was collected must be protected by an approved transfer mechanism, such as standard contractual clauses. Consult the Privacy team before setting up any new cross-border transfer.

### 10.4 Sharing with authorities

Requests from law enforcement or government agencies for personal data must be forwarded immediately to Legal. Employees must not respond to them directly.

## 11. Personal Data Breaches

### 11.1 Reporting timelines

- Report any suspected security incident to Security within 1 hour of discovery, as required by SEC-001 (security@lumora.example, #sec-incidents, or 1-512-555-0199).
- Personal data breaches must be reported to the privacy team within 24 hours of discovery. In practice, Security forwards incidents involving personal data to privacy@lumora.example, but anyone who discovers a personal data breach may also contact the Privacy team directly.
- Where required by law, regulators are notified within 72 hours of Lumora becoming aware of the breach. The DPO decides whether notification is required.

### 11.2 Breach response steps

1. Contain the breach (Security).
2. Assess what personal data was involved, how many individuals are affected, and the likely risk to them (Security and Privacy).
3. Decide on notifications to regulators, affected individuals, and affected customers (DPO).
4. Notify customers as required by their DPAs when their data is affected.
5. Document the breach, the decisions made, and the lessons learned, even when no notification is required.

### 11.3 Examples of personal data breaches

- An email containing employee salary data sent to the wrong person.
- A lost unencrypted device containing customer contact lists.
- Unauthorized access to a customer account in the Lumora platform.
- A vendor reports that its systems holding Lumora personal data were compromised.

## 12. Training and Awareness

All workforce members must complete privacy training annually. New hires complete privacy training within their first 30 days as part of mandatory onboarding training (see HR-008 Onboarding & Offboarding Procedures). Teams that handle sensitive personal data may be assigned additional role-based training.

## 13. Employee Privacy

Lumora respects employee privacy while operating its business. Employees should be aware that Lumora may monitor company systems as described in SEC-002 IT Acceptable Use Policy, and that there is no expectation of privacy on company devices. Employees may exercise their own privacy rights by contacting privacy@lumora.example or hr@lumora.example.

## 14. Enforcement and Reporting Concerns

Violations of this policy may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment or contract. Privacy concerns may be raised with the Privacy team, with HR, or anonymously through the Ethics & Compliance hotline at 1-800-555-0142. Lumora strictly prohibits retaliation for good-faith reports.

## 15. Frequently Asked Questions

### 15.1 A customer's end user emailed me asking us to delete their data. What do I do?

Forward the email to privacy@lumora.example within 2 business days of receipt. Do not delete anything yourself. The Privacy team will verify the request and coordinate with the customer.

### 15.2 How long does Lumora have to respond to a data subject request?

Access, deletion, and correction requests are answered within 30 days.

### 15.3 I accidentally emailed a spreadsheet with customer contacts to the wrong external address. Who do I tell?

Report it to Security within 1 hour (security@lumora.example, #sec-incidents, or 1-512-555-0199). Because it involves personal data, it must also reach the Privacy team within 24 hours; Security will forward it, or you may contact privacy@lumora.example directly.

### 15.4 Do I need a PIA for a new survey tool?

Yes, if the tool will collect or handle personal data. A PIA is required before launching any new processing of personal data or a new vendor handling personal data. The vendor must also sign a DPA.

### 15.5 How long do we keep data about candidates we did not hire?

Recruiting data of unsuccessful candidates is kept for 2 years and then deleted.

### 15.6 What happens to a customer's data when their contract ends?

It is deleted within 90 days after contract end.

### 15.7 How long are former employees' HR records kept?

Employee HR records are kept for 7 years after separation.
